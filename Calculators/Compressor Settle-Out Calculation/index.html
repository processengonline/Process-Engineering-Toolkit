Compressor Settle-Out Pressure & Temperature

Computes the equalized pressure and temperature reached when a compressor trips and its suction and discharge volumes become isolated (block valves closed, check valve open/leaking through the stopped machine) — the governing case for casing MAWP, dry gas seal reference pressure, spec-break/piping-class rating, and blowdown-system design. Uses real-gas mass inventory (P·V = Z·n·R·T) in each isolated volume, a mass-weighted internal-energy balance for the mixed temperature, and iterative closure on the settle-out Z-factor via the Dranchuk–Abou-Kassem correlation.

Not a substitute for a certified settle-out study. This tool assumes: (1) the combined system is a rigid, adiabatic, closed volume during equalization — no heat loss to piping/vessel walls (conservative — gives the highest settle-out temperature) and no shaft work; (2) constant, equal ideal-gas Cv for the energy balance even though real-gas Z is used for the mass inventory (the standard industry screening simplification); (3) sweet-gas Standing/DAK correlations unless you enter simulator-derived Pc/Tc directly — no Wichert–Aziz sour-gas (H₂S/CO₂) correction is applied; (4) a single common gas composition across all isolated volumes. For an actual EPC deliverable you still need: isolated-volume takeoffs confirmed against the P&ID (piping, vessels, coolers, compressor internals up to each closed valve), a process-simulator (HYSYS/UniSim/PROSIM) cross-check of Z and settle-out T/P, review of the valve isolation philosophy and trip logic, and sign-off by a licensed engineer. Treat this as a rigorous screening tool, not the calculation package itself.

Gas Properties & Z-Factor Method

Gas Composition

Use the process gas MW at the compressor suction (from simulation/gas analysis).

Z-Factor Basis

Real-gas Z is solved with the Dranchuk–Abou-Kassem (DAK) correlation, valid for 0.2 ≤ Ppr < 30 and 1.0 < Tpr ≤ 3.0.

Suction (Low-Pressure) Isolated Volume

Sum of suction piping, KO drum/scrubber, and compressor internal volume up to the closed suction block valve, per the P&ID.

Discharge (High-Pressure) Isolated Volume

Sum of discharge piping, cooler, and compressor internal volume up to the closed discharge block/check valve.

Design Check

MAWP of the compressor casing, dry gas seal reference/leak-off system rating, or the lowest piping class rating exposed to settle-out pressure across the train. Compared against Pso below.

Mass inventory (each isolated volume, real gas): ni = Pi·Vi / (Zi·R·Ti), with Pi absolute and R = 8314.462618 J/(kmol·K). mi = ni·MW.
Settle-out temperature (mass/mole-weighted internal-energy balance, constant Cv, no heat loss, no shaft work): Tso = Σ(ni·Ti) / Σni. This is exact for an ideal gas with equal, temperature-independent Cv on both sides — the standard first-pass simplification for real gas too, since the departure-function correction is second order for this purpose.
Settle-out pressure (real gas closure, iterative): Pso = Σni·Zso·R·Tso / ΣVi, solved together with Zso = f(Pso/Pc, Tso/Tc) until converged (successive substitution with under-relaxation).
Z-factor — Dranchuk & Abou-Kassem (1975): Z = 1 + (A1+A2/Tpr+A3/Tpr³+A4/Tpr⁴+A5/Tpr⁵)ρr + (A6+A7/Tpr+A8/Tpr²)ρr² − A9(A7/Tpr+A8/Tpr²)ρr⁵ + A10(1+A11ρr²)(ρr²/Tpr³)exp(−A11ρr²), with ρr = 0.27·Ppr/(Z·Tpr) solved implicitly. Constants A1…A11 = 0.3265, −1.0700, −0.5339, 0.01569, −0.05165, 0.5475, −0.7361, 0.1844, 0.1056, 0.6134, 0.7210.
Pseudo-critical properties (Standing, sweet/miscellaneous gas systems): Tpc(°R) = 168 + 325·SG − 12.5·SG², Ppc(psia) = 677 + 15·SG − 37.5·SG², SG = MW/28.9647 (relative to air), converted to K and bar abs for use above. Enter Pc/Tc directly from a process simulator for sour or non-typical gas mixtures — no Wichert–Aziz correction is applied here.
Design check: settle-out pressure is compared against the entered casing/seal/piping design pressure. A WARN means the settle-out case governs equipment rating and needs review (higher casing MAWP class, dry gas seal reference-pressure rating, blowdown/recycle-valve response time, or automatic depressurization on trip).
Scope note: this covers the common two-(or three-)volume single settle-out case. Multi-body trains with sequential isolation (e.g. independent trip of each casing) settle out in stages and need the same method applied per isolated section — repeat the calculation for each pair/set of volumes that equalizes together.
