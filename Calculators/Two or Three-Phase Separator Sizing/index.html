Separator Sizing — Souders-Brown Gas Capacity · Liquid Retention Time

Two/Three-Phase Separator Sizing Calculator

Sizes a vertical or horizontal gravity separator on the two classical governing checks: gas-capacity (Souders-Brown terminal droplet velocity, K-factor) setting the minimum vessel diameter for vapor/liquid disengagement, and liquid retention time setting the liquid holdup volume for level-control response. Includes an optional simplified free-water boot for three-phase service. Gas density uses the same real-gas Dranchuk–Abou-Kassem/Standing Z-factor logic as the Compressor Settle-Out calculator.

Not a substitute for a certified vessel sizing package. This tool assumes: (1) a single design K-factor per the Souders-Brown correlation, entered by you — K genuinely varies with vendor, mist-eliminator type, pressure, and foaming tendency, and is not hardcoded here; (2) the horizontal case is solved at the standard simplifying assumption of 50% normal liquid level (vapor and liquid cross-sections each = πD²/8) — a different design level changes both required D and L; (3) vessel tan-tan length excludes exact 2:1 ellipsoidal/hemispherical head volumes — heads are noted as additional length, not modeled precisely; (4) the free-water boot (if enabled) is sized on retention volume alone — it does not model interface level control range, weir design, oil-in-water/water-in-oil carryover, or boot nozzle/elevation requirements. For an actual EPC deliverable you still need: a process-simulator/vendor internals check, mist-eliminator or vane-pack vendor data for K, nozzle and instrument connection sizing, mechanical design (heads, supports, wall thickness) per ASME VIII, and review/sign-off by a licensed engineer. Treat this as a rigorous screening tool, not the calculation package itself.

Gas Stream & Z-Factor Method

Gas Properties

Enter mass flow directly — volumetric flow at operating conditions (Qgas) is computed internally from ρV below, avoiding double entry.
Informational only — not used in the Souders-Brown gas-capacity calculation below (that is governed by the K-factor, not k). Carried through for reference against relief/control-valve sizing on the same stream.

Z-Factor Basis

Real-gas Z is solved with the Dranchuk–Abou-Kassem (DAK) correlation, valid for 0.2 ≤ Ppr < 30 and 1.0 < Tpr ≤ 3.0.

Operating Conditions

Liquid Stream — Retention Time Basis

Typical 3–5 min for simple gravity separation with straightforward level control; more (5–10+ min) for surge/slug-capacity duty or slower level-control response — confirm against the project process design basis. Vliquid = Qliquid(m³/h)/3600 · tretention(s).

Gas Capacity — Souders-Brown

Typical 0.107 m/s (0.35 ft/s) for a vertical vessel without a mist eliminator, up to ~0.15 m/s (0.5 ft/s) or higher with a wire-mesh mist eliminator pad — confirm against GPSA Engineering Data Book Fig. 7-14 or vendor mist-eliminator data for the actual design. Vmax = K·√((ρL−ρV)/ρV).

Vessel Geometry — Vertical

Minimum vapor space above the high liquid level for droplet disengagement plus mist-eliminator clearance. Suggested starting value = max(0.5·D, 0.6 m), computed live from the required diameter below — uncheck the box above to override with a vendor/project figure.

Horizontal sizing uses the standard simplifying assumption of a 50% normal liquid level: at that level the vapor-space cross-section and the liquid cross-section are each exactly half the circle (πD²/8). Diameter is set by the gas-capacity constraint at that cross-section; length is then set by the liquid retention volume at that same diameter. No separate freeboard input is needed for this case.

Third Phase — Free Water

Water/boot retention is often longer than the primary liquid retention, typical 5–10+ min — confirm against the project process design basis.
Boot diameter is usually set by nozzle/instrumentation/vendor standard practice rather than derived from process. Hboot = Vwater / (πDboot²/4).
Simplified boot sizing only. This computes a retention-volume height for a small vertical boot cylinder — it does not model interface level control range, weir design, oil-in-water/water-in-oil carryover, or boot nozzle/elevation requirements. A full 3-phase design needs a vendor package or dedicated internals design.

Gas density (real gas, DAK/Standing — same method as the Compressor Settle-Out calculator): ρV = Pabs·1e5·MW / (Z·R·T), R = 8314.462618 J/(kmol·K). Z = 1.0 (ideal) or solved with Dranchuk–Abou-Kassem from Ppr = Pabs/Pc, Tpr = T/Tc, with Pc/Tc from Standing's sweet-gas correlation (Tpc(°R) = 168 + 325·SG − 12.5·SG², Ppc(psia) = 677 + 15·SG − 37.5·SG², SG = MW/28.9647) or entered directly from a process simulator. DAK constants A1…A11 = 0.3265, −1.0700, −0.5339, 0.01569, −0.05165, 0.5475, −0.7361, 0.1844, 0.1056, 0.6134, 0.7210, solved implicitly on ρr = 0.27·Ppr/(Z·Tpr).
Gas capacity (Souders-Brown): Vmax(m/s) = K·√((ρL−ρV)/ρV). K is a design coefficient — not fixed here — typical 0.107 m/s without a mist eliminator, up to ~0.15 m/s or higher with one; confirm against GPSA Fig. 7-14 or vendor mist-eliminator data.
Liquid retention volume: Vliquid = Qliquid(m³/h)/3600 · tretention(s), tretention entered in minutes.
Vertical sizing: D = √(4·Qgas/(π·Vmax)), governed by gas capacity alone. Hliquid = Vliquid/(πD²/4) at that same diameter. Tan-tan length L ≈ Hliquid (at normal level) + vapor disengagement/freeboard height (user input, suggested ≥ max(0.5·D, 0.6 m)) — ellipsoidal/hemispherical heads are additional length, not modeled precisely. L/D is reported against the commonly cited typical range of roughly 2.5–4:1 as an informational flag, not a hard fail.
Horizontal sizing: solved at the standard simplifying assumption of 50% normal liquid level, where vapor-space and liquid cross-sections are each πD²/8. Gas-capacity constraint sets the minimum diameter: D = √(8·Qgas/(π·Vmax)). Liquid retention then sets the length at that diameter: L = Vliquid/(πD²/8). L/D is reported against a commonly cited typical range of roughly 2.5–5:1 as an informational flag — a value far outside this range (e.g. very small L/D, meaning gas capacity alone is driving an oversized diameter with little length needed for retention) is noted so the engineer can iterate diameter against project L/D preference; this tool does not auto-iterate to force a "nice" L/D.
Free-water boot (optional, simplified): Vwater = Qwater(m³/h) · twater(min)/60. Hboot = Vwater/(πDboot²/4), with boot diameter entered directly (nozzle/vendor-practice driven). This sizes retention volume only — see the in-page scope note for what it excludes.
