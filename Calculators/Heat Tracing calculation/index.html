Heat Tracing — Rev. 1 · Screening / Winterization Basis

Heat Tracing / Winterization Load Calculator

Sizes electrical or steam heat tracing for an insulated pipe to maintain a target contents temperature against a design minimum ambient condition, using classic 1-D steady-state radial conduction through the insulation plus an outside film coefficient.

Screening-level tool, not a certified heat-tracing design. Pipe wall metal conduction resistance is neglected (small compared to insulation resistance) — a standard simplifying assumption for this class of calculation. This gives the theoretical steady-state heat loss for a uniformly insulated, uniformly exposed straight run at the design ambient condition only. It does not capture: transient heat-up/start-up loads, additional heat sinks at valves, flanges, supports, pipe shoes and instrument take-offs (these need separate allowances), insulation performance degraded by moisture ingress or damage, or a specific cable/tube's actual manufacturer power-output-vs-temperature curve. Confirm final circuit/tube layout, heat-sink allowances and cable selection against the project's heat-tracing design basis (e.g. IEEE 515 for electrical tracing) and the vendor's certified output data.

Pipe, Insulation & Design Basis

Pipe & Insulation Geometry

Inner radius of the insulation annulus — use the actual pipe OD (or OD over any existing jacketing) at the tracing location.
Typical mineral wool/fiberglass insulation ≈ 0.035–0.045 W/(m·K) at typical mean temperature; calcium silicate ≈ 0.055–0.065; cellular glass ≈ 0.04–0.05 — use the actual manufacturer's k at the relevant mean temperature for final design, conductivity rises with temperature.
Typical still-air ≈ 5–10 W/m²K, light wind ≈ 15–25, high wind/exposed ≈ 25–40 W/m²K — use the project's design wind speed basis; for insulated pipe with a metal jacket the film coefficient has a modest effect compared to insulation resistance, but include it for completeness.

Temperature & Design Basis

Freeze protection: typically a few degrees above 0 °C. Process maintenance: the required minimum process temperature.
Project-specific design value from the site winterization basis — this varies hugely by climate/site and must be taken from the actual project design basis, not assumed.
Typical design margin 1.2–1.5 to cover startup/heat-up transients, insulation aging/moisture ingress, and installation losses — confirm against project heat-tracing design basis (e.g. IEEE 515 practice for electrical tracing).
Enter 0 to see only per-metre results. Uniform straight-run assumption — does not include extra length/loss at valves, flanges, supports, etc.

Electrical Trace Cable

Self-regulating or constant-wattage cable output at the pipe's maintain temperature — take from the manufacturer's power-output-vs-temperature curve for the specific cable selected; output derates as temperature rises, this is NOT a fixed number across all conditions.

Steam Tracer

Take from steam tables at the actual tracing steam supply pressure — e.g. roughly 2100–2200 kJ/kg for typical 3–7 barg LP tracing steam, confirm against actual steam tables for the design pressure.
Optional — steam tracer tubes (typically 3/8"–1/2") are usually sized by number of parallel tubes needed for coverage/uniformity more than by a strict heat-duty-per-tube limit. Enter a target flow per tube only if you want a rough tube-count estimate.

Geometry: insulation outer diameter Dins (mm) = Dpipe + 2·tins.
Thermal resistance, per unit pipe length (m·K/W): pipe wall conduction resistance is neglected (negligible vs. insulation for this screening calc). Rinsulation = ln(Dins/Dpipe) / (2π·kins). Routside film = 1 / (hout·π·Dins[m]). Rtotal = Rinsulation + Routside film.
Heat loss: Qloss (W/m) = (Tmaintain − Tambient,design) / Rtotal, evaluated at the project's design minimum ambient temperature (steady-state, linear in ΔT for fixed resistance).
Design output: Required design heat output per metre = Qloss × design/safety factor (typ. 1.2–1.5).
Electrical tracing: parallel cables required per metre = ceil(required design output ÷ cable rated output at maintain temperature). Applied uniformly per unit length along a straight run — valves, flanges, supports and other heat sinks are NOT captured and need separate allowances. Total power (kW) = required output per metre × pipe run length L, if L is entered.
Steam tracing: required steam mass flow (kg/h per metre) = (required design output [W/m] × 3.6) ÷ hfg [kJ/kg]. Total steam flow (kg/h) = flow per metre × L, if L is entered. Optional tracer-tube count = ceil(flow per metre ÷ target per-tube capacity), a rough coverage estimate only.
