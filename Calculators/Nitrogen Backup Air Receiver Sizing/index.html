Nitrogen Backup Air Receiver Sizing

Sizes a high-pressure nitrogen cylinder/tube bank that backs up critical and ESD instrument air through a pressure-reducing regulator when the plant instrument-air compressors are lost. Uses the standard isothermal free-gas blowdown method (the same math as an instrument-air-receiver backup calc) applied to the bottle bank between its charged pressure and the regulator's minimum functional inlet pressure, with an optional real-gas (DAK Z-factor) correction for high storage pressure.

Screening-level inventory sizing only. This tool computes the standard isothermal free-gas blowdown result — it does not replace: the plant's SIS/ESD philosophy determination of the required backup duration; the regulator/PRV skid vendor's own sizing (droop curve, Cv, response/opening time under the changeover transient); a full changeover valve and cause-and-effect/logic design; or transportable-pressure-vessel code compliance and requalification for the cylinders/tubes themselves (e.g. relevant DOT/TPED/national cylinder regulations). Confirm required duration and demand against the project ESD/SIS philosophy and instrument air study before finalizing bottle count.

Critical / ESD N₂ Demand & Duration

Enter only the demand of critical/ESD instruments that must stay live on N₂ backup (e.g. ESD valve actuators, critical trip transmitters purge) — not the whole plant's normal instrument-air consumption. "Free" gas is referenced to the atmospheric pressure entered below.
This is commonly longer than a plain instrument-air-receiver holdup, since N₂ backup is often sized to cover the time to restore compressed-air service or to reach a safe shutdown/de-energized state. Confirm the value against the project's ESD/SIS philosophy — no single duration applies universally.

Real-Gas Correction (Z-Factor)

The basic Vbottles formula above is an ideal-gas screening result. At high storage pressure (150–300 barg) real-gas deviation from ideal is non-trivial for N₂ — enable this to correct via actual Z-factors at Ps1 and Ps2.
Pre-filled with nitrogen's actual critical properties (Tc = 126.2 K, Pc = 33.9 bar abs) — direct entry is used rather than a hydrocarbon-gravity correlation because N₂ is a fixed, known pure component, so its real critical properties are more accurate than a gas-gravity estimate. Z is solved with the same DAK correlation used elsewhere in this tool suite, valid for 0.2 ≤ Ppr < 30 and 1.0 < Tpr ≤ 3.0.

Joule–Thomson cooling / icing. Rapid discharge of high-pressure N₂ causes significant gas cooling at the regulator and downstream piping. Sustained high flow may require heat tracing, an ambient/electric vaporizer, or regulator heater sizing not covered by this screening tool — verify against the changeover flow rate and duration with the regulator vendor.
Ideal-gas (free-gas) blowdown sizing: Vbottles = t·Patm·Qfree / (Ps1 − Ps2), the standard isothermal blowdown result — the bottle bank volume that, discharged between its charged pressure Ps1 and the regulator's minimum functional inlet pressure Ps2, delivers Qfree·t of gas referenced to Patm. Identical in form to the instrument-air-receiver backup calculation, applied here to the high-pressure bottle bank instead of a low-pressure receiver.
Real-gas correction (when enabled): moles stored per unit bottle volume at pressure P and storage temperature T is n/V = P/(Z·R·T). The ideal formula implicitly assumes the moles released per unit volume are (Ps1−Ps2)/(R·T); the real-gas value is (Ps1/Z1 − Ps2/Z2)/(R·T). Sizing the bottle volume to release the same total moles required by the ideal free-gas demand in both cases gives Vbottles,real = Vbottles,ideal · (Ps1−Ps2) / (Ps1/Z1 − Ps2/Z2), with Ps1, Ps2 absolute and Z1, Z2 evaluated at Ps1 and Ps2 respectively (same storage temperature, isothermal). When Z < 1 the gas is denser than ideal at that condition (more moles per unit volume), so the required bottle volume is smaller than the ideal case for the same demand; when Z > 1 it is larger. For N₂ at typical ambient storage temperature the correction is a modest single-digit-percent adjustment across the 150–300 barg range — but it is not always negligible and is shown explicitly here rather than assumed away.
Z-factor — Dranchuk & Abou-Kassem (1975): Z = 1 + (A1+A2/Tpr+A3/Tpr³+A4/Tpr⁴+A5/Tpr⁵)ρr + (A6+A7/Tpr+A8/Tpr²)ρr² − A9(A7/Tpr+A8/Tpr²)ρr⁵ + A10(1+A11ρr²)(ρr²/Tpr³)exp(−A11ρr²), with ρr = 0.27·Ppr/(Z·Tpr) solved implicitly. Constants A1…A11 = 0.3265, −1.0700, −0.5339, 0.01569, −0.05165, 0.5475, −0.7361, 0.1844, 0.1056, 0.6134, 0.7210. Applied here with nitrogen's own critical properties (Tc = 126.2 K, Pc = 33.9 bar abs) rather than a gas-gravity correlation, since N₂ composition does not vary component-to-component the way a mixed process gas does.
Total stored mass: n = Ps1·Vbottles / (Z1·R·Tstorage), m = n·MW (MWN2 = 28.01 kg/kmol), R = 8314.462618 J/(kmol·K) — ideal gas (Z1 = 1) unless the real-gas correction is enabled.
Regulator inlet margin check: a WARN is shown if Ps2 is entered at less than 1.3× the delivery/set pressure to the IA header. Spring/dome-loaded pressure-reducing regulators typically need a meaningful inlet-to-outlet differential to hold set pressure accurately (droop); this is a rule-of-thumb screening flag only — check the regulator vendor's droop curve/minimum-inlet-pressure data for the specific device selected, not a fixed universal ratio.
Scope note: this sizes bottle-bank inventory only. It does not size the changeover valve, the regulator/PRV skid itself (Cv, droop, response time), heat tracing or vaporizer duty for cold discharge, or verify cylinder/tube transportable-pressure-vessel code compliance — see the note at the top of the page.
