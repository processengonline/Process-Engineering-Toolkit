Pump Hydraulics — NPSH Screening

Pump NPSH Available Calculator

Computes NPSH available at the pump suction flange from source vessel conditions, static elevation, and suction-line friction loss (Darcy–Weisbach, computed directly here), and checks the margin against the vendor's required NPSH.

Vapor pressure is a required input, not a correlation output. Pv is fluid- and temperature-specific — take it from process data / a physical-property package at the actual pumping temperature; this tool will not estimate it for you. Elevation Hs should be measured from the minimum operating liquid level in the source vessel to the pump suction centerline — that is the governing (worst-case) level for NPSH and is standard practice, even though it is conservative for other checks (e.g. vortexing) done elsewhere. Friction factor uses the Swamee–Jain explicit fit to Colebrook–White (turbulent) or f = 64/Re (laminar) — no fitting-K table is built in; add elbows/tees/strainer as an equivalent length, commonly estimated at 20–40 pipe diameters each — confirm against project piping data or a vendor K-table. The minimum-margin and NPSHa/NPSHr ratio checks below use your entered value, not a single hardcoded rule — the actual required margin depends on pump specific speed, service, and suction energy per Hydraulic Institute (ANSI/HI 9.6.1) guidance and project specification, and can exceed both the classic 0.6 m rule-of-thumb and a 1.1–1.3 ratio guideline for high-energy or high-Ns pumps. Not a substitute for the vendor's certified NPSHr curve or a full suction-system hydraulic study.

READY
NPSHa (m of liquid): NPSHa = (Psource,abs − Pv,abs)·10&sup5;/(ρ·g) + Hs − hf, with g = 9.80665 m/s². Psource,abs = gauge pressure at the source liquid surface + atmospheric pressure (negative gauge supported for vacuum sources). Hs is the signed elevation head from the minimum operating liquid level to the pump suction centerline: positive when the source is above the pump (flooded, adds to NPSHa), negative when the pump lifts liquid from below (suction lift, subtracts from NPSHa).
Suction-line friction loss (Darcy–Weisbach, computed directly — no separate line-sizing tool required): v = Q/(3600·πD²/4); Re = ρ·v·D/μ (μ in Pa·s); f = 64/Re for Re < 2300 (laminar), else Swamee–Jain f = 0.25/[log10(ε/(3.7D) + 5.74/Re0.9)]² (turbulent, explicit fit to Colebrook–White); hfpipe(m) = f·(L+Lequiv)/D · v²/(2g). Total hf = hfpipe + any additional fixed losses entered directly (e.g. a strainer or suction valve ΔP converted to head).
Margin check: both the absolute margin (NPSHa − NPSHr) and the ratio (NPSHa/NPSHr) are shown; the PASS/WARN badge is driven by your entered minimum-margin value, not a single universal number — ANSI/HI 9.6.1 ties the actual required margin to pump suction specific speed and service, and commonly cited absolute (≥0.6 m) and ratio (≥1.1–1.3) guidelines are minimums for ordinary services only.
Not a substitute for the vendor's certified NPSHr curve, a full suction-system hydraulic study, or review/sign-off by a licensed engineer. See the note at the top of the page.
