Instrument Air Receiver Sizing

Sizes the instrument air receiver vessel required to hold enough reserve air to keep the instrument air header above a minimum functional pressure for a specified ride-through time after all air compressors trip, per ISA-7.0.01 plant air-quality/reliability practice and the standard CAGI/Atlas Copco isothermal free-air blowdown method. Reports the required volume, the nearest standard vendor package size, reserve air mass, and an L/D-based vessel geometry estimate.

Screening-level sizing only. This tool uses the standard isothermal free-air blowdown mass balance (referenced to Nm³/normal conditions) and does not replace: the project's instrument air philosophy document, a full ISA-7.0.01 compliance review, compressor start/cycling-frequency sizing (a separate criterion from backup holdup time — governed by compressor manufacturer minimum-cycle-time limits, not just outage ride-through), relief/PSV sizing for the receiver itself, or ASME Section VIII vessel code design by a certified vessel designer.

Backup Air Demand & Ride-Through Time

Instrument Air Demand During Backup

Air demand that must be sustained purely from receiver inventory after a compressor trip — normally the continuous consumption of connected pneumatic control valves/positioners, not total plant demand.
Converted internally to Nm³/min for the sizing equation.

Required Backup / Ride-Through Time

Typical EPC client specs commonly require 10–30 minutes of instrument air backup after loss of all compressors — this varies by project instrument air philosophy and the applicable ISA-7.0.01 requirement. Confirm against the actual project spec; do not assume one universal number.

Header Pressure Limits

Normal operating pressure of the receiver, set by the compressor discharge/unloading control.
Typically the highest minimum supply pressure required by any connected pneumatic actuator/positioner in the system, per vendor datasheets.
Basis for the Nm³ ("free air") definition used for the demand flow and reserve-air-mass results below.

Required receiver volume (isothermal free-air blowdown, standard industry form): V = t·Patm·Qfree / (P1 − P2), with P1 and P2 absolute and Qfree expressed as free air (Nm³/min) referenced to Patm and Tstd. This is the standard CAGI/Atlas Copco-style, ISA-7.0.01-practice receiver-sizing equation — an isothermal, ideal-gas mass balance on the receiver as it blows down from the charged pressure P1 to the minimum allowable header pressure P2 while continuously supplying Qfree.
Temperature correction (optional, off by default): the free-air Nm³ definition fixes a reference temperature Tstd. If the receiver's actual gas temperature differs materially from Tstd, the physical volume needed to hold the same standard mass changes proportionally: Vcorrected = V × (Treceiver / Tstd), both in absolute temperature. With the toggle off, Treceiver ≈ Tstd is assumed — the standard simplification used by most receiver-sizing guides.
Reserve air mass: the free-air volume released between P1 and P2 (Nm³ = Qfree·t) is converted to mass using the ideal-gas density at the standard reference (Patm, Tstd) with MWair = 28.97 g/mol: m = (Qfree·t) × [Patm·MWair / (R·Tstd)]. Ideal gas (Z = 1) is an appropriate, standard simplification at the modest pressures typical of instrument air systems.
Standard package rounding: the required volume is rounded up to the nearest of a list of common vendor package receiver sizes (300–20,000 L). These are illustrative common sizes only, not a rule — the actual vessel is custom to the project scope and vendor standard range.
Vessel L/D geometry: for a cylindrical vessel with 2:1 semi-elliptical heads (head volume ≈ 0.215·D³ each), V = (π/4)·D²·Lcyl + 2·(0.215·D³), with Lcyl = (L/D)·D, solved for D in closed form. Reported diameter and tan-tan (cylindrical shell) length are computed from the selected standard package volume — or the raw required volume if it exceeds the largest standard package listed.
Design check: compares the charged pressure P1, plus an allowance for expected pressure rise from compressor unloading/recharge overshoot, against the entered receiver design pressure. A WARN means the design pressure needs review against actual compressor control setpoints.
Scope note: this tool sizes for backup ride-through holdup only. It does not size the receiver for compressor start/cycling frequency (a separate, often more limiting, criterion governed by the compressor manufacturer's minimum-cycle-time requirement), nor for receiver relief/PSV protection, nor for vessel mechanical/code design.
References: ISA-7.0.01-1996 (R2006) Quality Standard for Instrument Air, CAGI (Compressed Air and Gas Institute) receiver-sizing guidance, Atlas Copco Compressed Air Manual, ASME Boiler & Pressure Vessel Code Section VIII (vessel design).
